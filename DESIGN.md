# Prototype design direction

This is a functional UI direction for a camera try-on prototype. It is **not a store brand guide**. Replace it when a real store name, product images, copy, and brand system are supplied.

- Audience: shoppers testing a garment preview on a phone.
- Purpose: get from an item to a usable camera view with as little browsing friction as possible.
- Voice: plain, specific, and candid about the limits of a 2D overlay.
- Energy 1 / Rhythm 2 / Motion 1: calm interface, a distinct catalog and fitting-room composition, and motion only for state changes.
- Palette: near-white for browsing, charcoal for text and camera framing, rust used sparingly for orientation and focus.
- Type: system sans serif for clear controls and product labels on small screens.
- Motif: registration corners in the camera placeholder echo the body framing instruction. They are not used as decoration elsewhere.
- Product illustrations: explicitly marked demonstration garments. No invented prices, materials, stock, sizes, or brand claims.

Why the first version changed: its fictional fashion brand, hero illustration, promotional slogans, badges, prices, and three-step explainer made it look like a finished store without real products. The product selection and live preview now carry the page.
